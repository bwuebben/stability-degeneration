## Reading the programme

Directories 01–04 contain the two-step foundations and their repeated-factor extension. Directories 05–07 develop three-step metrics and local moduli geometry; 08–09 form the dynamical pair. Directory 10 proves a general singular-graded obstruction on compact Kähler surfaces. Directory 11 studies mass increase and acyclic cancellation for phase flows on quiver complexes. Directory 12 constructs harmonic norm paths and labelled honeycombs from full boundary norms. Directory 13 applies the stationary and quotient-metric methods to local rank-three K3 bundle moduli. Directory 14 proves a general local harmonic/quiver metric comparison on projective K3 surfaces and applies it to a rank-five, two-parameter wall crossing. This numbering follows the development and mathematical dependencies of the programme and supplies a reading order; it is not part of the manuscript titles.

The bipartite spectral theorem and the toric chamber constructions give independent analytic and geometric starting points. The two-step metric-response paper joins them on the same tangent bundles. The repeated-factor spectral paper replaces scalar graph directions by matrix modes within its two-step class.

The three-step metric paper treats composable extensions with integrability relations and compares actual metrics and every small spectral scale with an independently balanced graph. The wall-moduli paper retains the full analytic obstruction and controls the harmonic L² metric on smooth pieces of the local quotient. The repeated-factor moduli paper develops the corresponding matrix estimates in a specified rank-five deformation problem. The K3 wall-crossing paper applies projection-relative balancing and complementary-gauge minimization to a complex twelve-dimensional bundle-moduli neighborhood. It compares actual harmonic metrics with cotangent-Grassmannian quotient metrics uniformly through stabilizer changes, giving collapse scales, complete rescaled limits and intrinsic distance asymptotics. This is a local application of the stationary and quotient-metric methods; global completion and bubbling remain outside its scope. The general K3 comparison retains all finite symmetry directions and allows arbitrary stabilizer changes, with linear spatial error unless a verified sign symmetry improves it to quadratic error. Its rank-five doubled-triangle example realizes five walls in two parameters. Intrinsic convergence through singular real levels yields complete rescaled limits under the stated convergence hypotheses, and selected transverse contractions have models T*P⁹, T*P¹¹ and T*P¹⁷. These are the stationary and moduli-geometric branches.

For dynamics, the initial-condition paper proves differentiable phase selection relative to a chosen invariant graph, with uniform exponential tracking near a stable family. The wall-and-unstable paper proves confinement in moving coefficients even when diagonal metrics diverge, and identifies a residual carrying initial conditions. The singular-graded paper proves a general rank-two surface obstruction: the squared norm on an equal-slope line decays strictly faster than a bounded-remainder extension of the categorical weight model would permit. The theorem includes nonreduced singularities and repeated line factors, with toric and K3 examples. Its stationary curvature theorem concerns the toric family.

The categorical phase-flow paper gives an explicit mass-increase example under the positivity-based phase convention, followed by cancellation from arbitrary positive metrics for invertible source differentials. For surjective source differentials and a large least initial acyclic singular value, its general mixed theorem allows arbitrary surviving tuples, unequal dimensions and fixed upper-half-plane charges; constants are uniform in arrow count, stabilizer and acyclic condition number with dimensions and norm bounds fixed. Relaxation follows separately in equal dimensions at charges −1+i and 1+i when the cohomology Kempf–Ness function has finite infimum. The balanced and Pauli cases are corollaries. Static nonscalar estimates and an explicit C2 limit with divergent Hölder Hessian seminorms complement the dynamics. Uniform final relaxation and general quotient geometry remain open.

The harmonic-path paper constructs a finite algebraically labelled path from every full split boundary triple in every finite rank for the specified A₂ representation, Novikov field, symmetric charges and radial area form. Equality uniqueness holds in the finite regular principal-velocity class with intrinsic first-order matching. General boundary continuity and a global Fukaya correspondence remain open.

These theorems retain their stated bundle, polarization and local-domain hypotheses. A general theory for arbitrary filtrations and stable factors remains open, as do repeated-factor dynamics and a replacement asymptotic law for singular graded sheaves. The surface obstruction does not contradict the weight-filtration theorem with locally free graded object.

## Build and reproduce

Each paper has a source and reproduction guide in its directory. Papers 01–03 and 05–10 have assembled, self-contained `paper.tex` files apart from their supplied figures. Papers 11–14 directly include their supplied sections and bibliography. Compile with `latexmk -pdf paper.tex`. The earlier repeated-factor spectral paper uses `main.tex` and `sh scripts/build.sh`.

Run `python3 reproduce.py` from the repository root to regenerate the finite computations and figures. Programs with third-party dependencies use `uv` and the paper's pinned `requirements.txt` or inline script dependency; the others use the Python standard library. The finite calculations establish the printed identities and examples, while the geometric, elliptic and parabolic assertions rely on the proofs. Released PDF hashes and archive identities are in `manuscripts.json`.

The Zenodo records preserve the released manuscripts. Papers 01–10 await arXiv submission.

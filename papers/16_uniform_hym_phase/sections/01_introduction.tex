\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Hermitian--Yang--Mills flow relates stability of a holomorphic bundle
to the geometry of its Hermitian metrics. In a deformation slice near a
polystable bundle, the flow has finitely many neutral directions and an elliptic
complement with a positive spectral gap. The neutral directions contain
the deformation and automorphism data. They may acquire arbitrarily
small positive scales after deformation, and their stabilizers may change.
The question considered here is whether an exact finite-dimensional
trajectory can be selected from the initial metric so that the remaining
metric error decays at the rate of the fixed elliptic complement.

There are two distinctions in this question. First, the selected
trajectory must solve the corrected finite equation on a parabolically
invariant graph. Its leading moment-map approximation need not give a
uniformly accurate comparison for all time. Second, harmonic deformation
coordinates alone do not determine a metric on a fixed holomorphic bundle.
At the undeformed point they are zero for an entire family of parallel
stationary metrics. The reconstructing complex automorphism must therefore
be retained. We prove phase selection in that group, with estimates in a
right-invariant metric, and then obtain an exponentially vanishing relative
error between actual HYM metrics.

\subsection{Geometric data and the local regime}
Let $(X,\omega_0)$ be a compact connected K\"ahler manifold, and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split}
 E_0=\bigoplus_{i=1}^\ell F_i\otimes W_i
\end{equation}
be a locally free polystable holomorphic bundle. The stable factors $F_i$
are mutually nonisomorphic and have the same slope at $\omega_0$;
the finite-dimensional multiplicity spaces $W_i$ are arbitrary.
Fix a direct-sum Hermitian--Einstein metric. Let $\omega_p$ be a smooth
finite-dimensional family of nearby K\"ahler forms, with $p=0$
corresponding to $\omega_0$. The parameter is constant along every flow.
Section~\ref{sec:coordinates} constructs smooth factor HE metrics $h_i(p)$.
We write $h_p=\bigoplus_i h_i(p)\otimes I$, and fix its HE determinant
metric throughout the flow. The factor HE constants are $\kappa_i(p)$;
the determinant-normalized constant for $E_0$ is $\kappa_E(p)$.

Put $r_i=\operatorname{rk}F_i$ and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:group}
 G=\left\{(g_i)\in\prod_i\mathrm{GL}(W_i):
                  \prod_i\det(g_i)^{r_i}=1\right\}^{0},
 \qquad K=G\cap\prod_i\mathrm U(W_i).
\end{equation}
These are parallel automorphisms of the split bundle.
Write $\ha=i\ka$ for the Hermitian real subspace of $\g$.
It has dimension $\sum_i(\dim W_i)^2-1$, independent of the
stabilizer of a deformation. Let $P_p$ be the $L^2(h_p,\omega_p)$
projection from trace-free Hermitian endomorphisms onto $\ha$, and
let $Q_p=I-P_p$.

Let $d=\ddbar_{E_0}$. Smooth equivariant identifications of its harmonic
spaces give a fixed complex vector space $V$, with harmonic inclusion
$\iota_p:V\longrightarrow\Omega^{0,1}(\End_0E_0)$.
The canonical convergent Kuranishi reconstruction and obstruction are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kuranishi}
 \begin{split}
 a_p(x)&=\iota_p x-d_p^*G_{2,p}\bigl(a_p(x)\wedge a_p(x)\bigr),\\
 \kappa_p(x)&=H_{2,p}\bigl(a_p(x)\wedge a_p(x)\bigr),\qquad
 Z_p=\kappa_p^{-1}(0).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $H_{2,p}$ and $G_{2,p}$ are the harmonic projection and Green
operator in degree two. We retain the full convergent equations.
The notation $gx$ denotes the induced representation of $G$, and
$C_x\xi=[\xi,x]$ its infinitesimal action. Unless an inclusion is needed,
we also write $x$ for $\iota_p x$.

For nonintegral $s>0$, $h^s$ denotes the closure of smooth sections in
the $C^s$ norm. All section norms use the split connection and metric;
the identifications in Section~\ref{sec:coordinates} make them uniformly
equivalent for small $p$. On $G$ fix a right-invariant Riemannian metric
whose inner product at the identity is $\Ad K$ invariant, and denote its
distance by $d_R$.

On a fixed holomorphic bundle $d_{x_b}=d+a_p(x_b)$, where $x_b\in Z_p$,
our coordinates for the metric are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric-coordinates}
 H=g^*(h_p e^u),\qquad x=gx_b,\qquad
 u\in Q_p h^{17/8}(\Herm_0E_0).
\end{equation}
The exact evolution of these coordinates is derived in
Proposition~\ref{prop:slice}. The coefficient $x$ may stay small while
$g$ and $g^{-1}$ become unbounded.

\begin{definition}\label{def:confined}
For a coefficient radius $r$, a solution is \emph{confined} if its
exact sliced harmonic coefficient satisfies $|x(s)|\le r$
throughout its maximal interval in the domain of the geometric
coordinate equations. The normal coordinate is initially small.
There is no bound on the reconstructing gauge.
\end{definition}

Confinement is an assumption on the actual coefficient trajectory.
The theorem below proves continuation in the geometric domain under
this assumption. It does not assert confinement for every nearby metric.

\subsection{Main theorem}
Use the geometric $L^2$ inner products on $V$ and $\ha$ to define
the Riesz representative of the quadratic moment map by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
 \langle\mu_p^\sharp(x),\xi\rangle_{\ha,p}
       =\operatorname{Re}\langle C_x\xi,x\rangle_{V,p}.
\end{equation}
This convention has no factor $1/2$. Define the central slope difference
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central}
 b_p|_{F_i\otimes W_i}=(\kappa_E(p)-\kappa_i(p))I.
\end{equation}
It belongs to $\ha$ and $b_0=0$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every prescribed finite $m\ge6$, there are a parameter neighborhood,
a radius $r>0$, a chosen jointly $C^m$, $K$-equivariant map
\[
 \Psi(p,\cdot):B_{4r}(V)\longrightarrow Q_p h^{19/8}(\Herm_0E_0),
\]
and positive constants $\delta,C,c$ with the following properties.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(i) Invariant graph.} The graph $u=\Psi(p,x)$ is locally invariant for the
exact sliced HYM equation, and
$\Psi(p,0)=D_x\Psi(p,0)=0$. Its exact finite evolution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective}
 \dot x=C_x\beta_0(p,x),\qquad
 \dot g=\beta_0(p,x)g,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_0(p,x)\in\ha$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-expansion}
 \beta_0(p,x)=b_p-\mu_p^\sharp(x)+E_p(x),\qquad
 |D_x^jE_p(x)|\le C|x|^{4-j}\quad(0\le j\le2).
\end{equation}
The leading coefficient field is
$-\nabla_V\bigl(\frac14\|\mu_p^\sharp-b_p\|_{\ha,p}^2\bigr)$.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(ii) Asymptotic phase and metric tracking.}
Fix $x_b\in Z_p\cap B_r(V)$ and initial data
\[
 g_0\in G,\quad x_0=g_0x_b\in B_r(V),\quad
 u_0\in Q_p h^{17/8},\quad
 D=\|u_0-\Psi(p,x_0)\|_{h^{17/8}}<\delta.
\]
Assume confinement in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:confined}.
The normalized HYM flow starting at $H^{\mathrm{in}}=g_0^*(h_pe^{u_0})$
then exists in these coordinates for all $s\ge0$. There is a selected
exact trajectory $(x_*,g_*)$ of \eqref{eq:effective}, with
$x_*(s)=g_*(s)x_b\in Z_p\cap B_{2r}(V)$, such that
\[
 H_*(s)=g_*(s)^*(h_pe^{\Psi(p,x_*(s))})
\]
is an exact HYM flow on $d_{x_b}$ and
\begin{align}
 \|u(s)-\Psi(p,x(s))\|_{h^{17/8}}&\le CD e^{-cs},\label{eq:normal-main}\\
 |x(s)-x_*(s)|+d_R(g(s),g_*(s))&\le CD e^{-cs},\label{eq:lift-main}\\
 e^{-CD e^{-cs}}H_*(s)\ \le H(s)&\le e^{CD e^{-cs}}H_*(s).
                                                        \label{eq:order-main}
\end{align}
More precisely, with $q(s)=g(s)g_*(s)^{-1}$ and
$v(s)=u(s)-\Psi(p,x(s))$, the centered relative logarithm
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-log}
 \log\!\left(
 e^{-\Psi(p,x_*)/2}q^{*h_p}e^{\Psi(p,x)+v}q
 e^{-\Psi(p,x_*)/2}\right)
\end{equation}
has $h^{17/8}$ norm at most $CD e^{-cs}$.

\smallskip\noindent
\textup{(iii) Dependence on initial data.} For fixed $p$, the selected initial phase
$(x_*(0),g_*(0))$ is the restriction of a $C^1$ map of the ambient
sliced initial data $(x_0,g_0,u_0)$, with uniform derivative bounds in
the indicated H\"older and right-trivialized group norms. These bounds
are uniform for small $p$. Nearby fixed-determinant metrics are covered
by the smooth charts of Proposition~\ref{prop:metric-charts}.

\smallskip\noindent
The constants use the fixed complementary elliptic gap and smooth
local bounds. They are independent of positive finite-dimensional spectral lower
bounds, ratios of positive eigenvalues of $C_x^*C_x$, and stabilizer
changes. They may depend on the fixed smooth background, $m$, the
parameter neighborhood and the confinement radii. The chosen finite
field has a complete extension agreeing with it on $B_{2r}$ whose
right-trivialized derivative bound is $L<c$. The selected lifted
trajectory is unique among trajectories of this extension that track
the actual lifted trajectory at some exponential rate faster than $L$.
\end{theorem}

The graph and its complete extension are choices. The theorem does not
assert a canonical phase independent of those choices, or differentiability
on a singular coarse moduli space. Its regularity statements are made
before quotienting, in ambient harmonic and metric coordinates.
The exponentially decaying quantity is the error transverse to the
selected finite motion. The finite trajectory itself may relax slowly.

\subsection{Predecessors and proof strategy}
Finite-dimensional models already play a substantial role in HYM
asymptotics. Haiden--Katzarkov--Kontsevich--Pandit
\cite{hkkp2018,hkkp2023} establish order comparison and algebraically
determined asymptotics in their curve and quiver settings. Lam
\cite[Theorem~8.2 and Proposition~8.6]{lam}
proves locally free graded asymptotics for semistable bundles,
under his stated rationality hypothesis, and bounded-distance
comparison with a reduced flow. His Kuranishi
construction retains the full nonabelian automorphism group and the
complete obstruction \cite[Theorem~8.7]{lam}. These aspects of the local
model are not additional assertions of the present theorem.

The comparison proved here selects an exact flow on a parabolically
invariant graph, depends $C^1$ on the initial metric, and has relative
error tending exponentially to zero. It does not supply the algebraic
iterated-filtration asymptotics of the corrected finite field, and thus
does not replace Lam's asymptotic classification. The two results
address different parts of the dynamics.

The abstract principles are also established. Simonett's
parameter-dependent invariant-manifold theorem \cite{simonett1994}
provides the graph after a common parabolic realization is verified.
Johnson--Latushkin--Schnaubelt \cite{jls} prove exact exponentially
tracking center trajectories in a parabolic setting. Differentiable
stable foliations and noncompact normally hyperbolic manifolds have
their own general theory \cite{psw,blz2000,blz2008}.
Our task is the geometric realization of these principles with the
entire parallel group retained, including uniform estimates on the
lifted phase and its reconstruction as a metric.
Equivariant local bundle models and nearby-polarization HYM theory
are developed by Buchdahl--Schumacher and Delloque
\cite{bs22,delloque2024,delloque2025}.

The proof has three main steps. We first derive an exact noncommuting
metric equation. Centering the derivative of the exponential removes
the variable second-order coefficients, and leaves a fixed elliptic
operator on the complement of all parallel Hermitian endomorphisms.
Its gap persists under the allowed parameters. This gives a chosen
invariant graph on the ambient harmonic space, while equivariance
preserves the full integrable locus.

We next retain the group variable in the asymptotic-phase argument.
Pulling the perturbed finite trajectory backward by the exact reduced
flow gives a curve of finite length in $V\times G$. Slow derivative
growth of the reduced flow and fast normal decay make both this curve
and its initial-data derivatives converge. The limiting phase lies
in $G$ itself, so it preserves the original holomorphic orbit without
inverting its possibly rank-changing differential.

Finally, the right quotient $gg_*^{-1}$ cancels the possibly large
reconstructing gauges in the relative metric. The same estimate
keeps the selected coefficient trajectory inside the geometric domain.
An inner-radius argument then covers nearby initial metrics.
The noncommuting bicubic example in Section~\ref{sec:application}
shows how full obstruction, multiplicities, changing stabilizers and
separated finite scales occur in one smooth geometric background.
Singular graded sheaves and collapsing backgrounds require additional
analytic estimates; no complementary-gap assertion for them is used here.

# Canonical metrics on singular moduli of K3 bundles

By [Bernd Johannes Wuebben](https://bwuebben.github.io/), [ORCID](https://orcid.org/0009-0002-4588-3284).
Preprint. Original date: 8 November 2026. This version: 8 November 2026. 17 pages.

[Read the paper](canonical-metrics-singular-k3-moduli.pdf) · [Abstract and citation](https://bwuebben.github.io/papers/canonical-metrics-singular-k3-moduli/) · [Zenodo:24060228](https://zenodo.org/records/24060228) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24060228)

For the stated twice-primitive Mukai vectors on a K3 surface of Picard rank one, a rank bound excludes non-locally-free semistable sheaves and slope Jordan–Hölder factors. The compact moduli spaces have positive-dimensional singular strata and admit neither a symplectic resolution nor a global smoothing. Their harmonic L² metric is the canonical singular Ricci-flat metric in a positive multiple of an ample determinant class. Holomorphic Ext-quiver charts give relative O(r²) approximation by flat hyperkähler quotients, uniformly through stabilizer changes within each fixed background chart. The regular-locus completion is the original moduli space, tangent cones are unique, and no singular point has an exact local cone isometry. On a quartic K3, the vector (5,H,0) gives an 18-dimensional example with singular strata of dimensions 12 and 6.

## Build

Run `latexmk -pdf main.tex` in this directory with a standard AMS LaTeX installation. The main file inputs the supplied sections and labelled bibliography. The checked-in PDF is the archived manuscript; its checksum appears in the repository's `manuscripts.json`.

The quantitative comparison uses the local metric theorems in [Quantitative metric wall crossing for K3 bundle moduli](https://bwuebben.github.io/papers/quiver-metric-wall-crossing/) and [Quantitative local models for Hermitian–Einstein metrics](https://bwuebben.github.io/papers/local-hym-reduction/). The present family has no bubbling; the theorem does not assert a global uniform chart radius, optimality of the exponent, or derivative asymptotics across lower strata.
